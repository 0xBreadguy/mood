# Product brief

mood is a minimal, experimental Omarchy plugin for choosing a musical
atmosphere and leaving it running while you work.

## Decisions

- The project is named **mood**, written lowercase.
- Prioritize a tight Omarchy plugin experience, with a compact shell-bar
  presence and a small mood picker when needed.
- The main surface communicates only **mood** and **playback state**. Song
  titles, artists, album art and a visible queue aren't central to the task.
- Merge the visual and the control. Avoid a separate transport-button row
  attached to an otherwise decorative animation.
- The plugin is one item on the bar, and that item is a visualizer. Click
  plays or pauses. Right-click opens a popup for choosing the mood.
- No word rests on the bar. After a mood switch, the mood's name appears
  briefly in place at the active mood's tine so the bar item widens only when
  needed for longer names.
- The bar visual is **Tine**: a row of thirteen tines in the popup's order,
  with the active mood standing on its tine and whole-comb motion that travels
  outward along the tines as audio plays. It offers three animation variants
  (**Resonance**, **Impulse**, and still **Steady**) and adapts to any
  highlight color.
- Back goes to the previous track that was actually heard.
- On first open, the popup highlights a compact two-column legend of the bar's
  gestures at the top ("Bar controls"). Dismissing it—or using a scroll
  gesture on the bar item—retires the top highlight so the popup opens
  straight onto the moods; the legend remains available inside collapsed
  Manual controls alongside the explicit Back, Play/Pause and Skip buttons.
- Choose a mood and play. Stream CC0 music that already exists on demand
  rather than generating audio locally, downloading collections in bulk, or
  hosting music ourselves.
- A mood is a category of songs in the catalog, and the names people flip
  through are those categories. The initial catalog combines Open Lo-Fi and
  three Alec Penn Radio genres from the Internet Archive (Lo-Fi,
  Neo-Classical, and Psychedelic Jazz): thirteen categories, 451 tracks total.
- Aim for restraint with subtle optionality: customizable highlight color
  (including picking a color off the screen), animation variant, scroll
  mapping, and media-key integration.
- Use simple, restrained animations to communicate states, driven by the
  plugin's own audio rather than system sound.
- Keep controls learnable and keyboard-accessible, with visible focus,
  meaningful accessible names, and state distinctions that survive animations
  being off.
- Develop the interface from this brief and general UI principles rather than
  existing music-player conventions.

## What is built

Built and checked on Omarchy 4.5.0 inside Omabox, a hidden desktop with
silent audio output. Automated checks verify playback state transitions,
fetching, UI interactions, and accessibility without audible playback; a full
listening pass across the 451 tracks has not been completed yet. Building a
default does not settle every open proposal; the sections below note what is
still open.

- A native Omarchy plugin, `0xbreadguy.mood`: a playback service and one bar
  item. Install and removal steps are in the [README](../README.md).
- A playback core with six states that change only on evidence: `stopped`,
  `loading`, `playing`, `paused`, `failed`, and `offline`. It plays one track
  at a time through `mpv`, keeps a shuffled queue and a listening history per
  mood, advances at the end of a track, coalesces quick skips, backs off on
  failure, and remembers only the last mood and first-open guide state.
  [Playback](playback.md) has the architecture and measured resource cost.
- A catalog of 451 track pointers in thirteen categories, generated by
  `tools/build_catalog.py` from the two collections' own metadata. The display
  names for the categories live in `catalog/categories.json` and remain open
  to revision.
- Audio features measured from the plugin's own stream: loudness, three
  frequency bands, and onsets, 25 times a second, scaled to each track.
- The interactions in the table below as defaults, including the right-button
  substitute for horizontal scroll on single-wheel mice.
- The popup as decided: a dismissible first-open Bar controls highlight, the
  thirteen moods, collapsed Manual controls (with the gesture legend tucked
  inside once dismissed), and collapsed Settings—all reachable by keyboard.
- A replaceable surface seam under `plugin/surfaces/`. The bar item's drawing
  and the popup's presentation are swapped as a unit, while playback, input,
  focus, accessibility, and settings stay in the plugin core. See
  [Surfaces](surfaces.md). `tine` is the plugin's look (whole-comb audio
  motion and in-place name reveal). `plain` is a neutral reference and
  fallback, not a design, and isn't offered in the popup.
- Settings for highlight color (preset or screen-picked custom color),
  animation variant, which scroll changes the mood, media keys, and surface
  selection when multiple looks are listed.

## Interaction proposal: skip and back

Click and right-click are decided, as above, and so is what back does. The
scroll and media-key bindings are implemented as defaults so they can be
tried; which scroll changes the mood is configurable via `moodScroll`.

| Input | Default behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Click | Play or pause |
| Scroll down | Next track within the mood |
| Scroll up | Previous track from actual listening history |
| Scroll sideways | Next or previous mood, with the name shown briefly in place |
| Right-click | Open the mood picker |
| Media keys | Play/pause, next and previous |

Sideways scroll changes the mood by default, while vertical scroll skips or
goes back within the mood. The `moodScroll` setting can swap them so plain
vertical scroll changes moods instead.

Shift+scroll was considered as the single-wheel mouse fallback for horizontal
scroll, but it cannot be implemented in this shell: the bar never holds
keyboard focus, so Hyprland does not report held modifier keys on scroll
events over the bar item. Instead, the plugin supports holding the right
mouse button while scrolling, which needs no keyboard.

Gestures are surfaced in the tooltip and the popup's first-open Bar controls
card, with explicit Back, Play/Pause, and Skip buttons kept inside collapsed
Manual controls. One trackpad gesture must not skip multiple tracks through
momentum, or skip a track and change the mood together. Back returns to the
previous track that was actually heard and starts it from the beginning
(resuming mid-track would require retaining the track's bytes after leaving
it).

The IPC commands are named `skip` and `back` rather than `next`/`previous` so
they cannot be confused with stepping to the next mood (`step 1` / `step -1`).

This interaction model favors individual track streams with a private queue
and history. A continuous live-radio stream cannot offer the same skip/back
behavior unless the source provides equivalent controls or the plugin buffers
the stream locally.

## Delivery architecture

Built in `catalog/catalog.json`, the plugin plays from a small list of track
pointers: for each track, its category, title, license, and where its bytes
live. A location is either a direct URL or a byte range inside a published
archive. Adding a collection means adding pointers and categories without
changing the plugin itself.

- **Archive byte ranges (Open Lo-Fi):** 166 MP3 tracks sit inside one 554 MB
  release ZIP. The fetcher retrieves a single track by explicit byte range,
  verifies the archive size and entry header on the first bytes, unpacks it
  in memory, and checks its checksum; see [music-sources.md](music-sources.md).
  This keeps the install small and writes no audio to disk, at the cost of
  depending on that published release asset.
- **Direct links (Alec Penn Radio on the Internet Archive):** 285 MP3 tracks
  across three genres, each with its own URL, size, duration, and CRC-32
  checksum from Archive metadata.

## Audio-responsive animation

The visual communicates playback state first, and decoded audio gently
modulates it while playing.

- Analyze the plugin's own decoded audio so system calls, notifications, and
  other apps never move the visual.
- Smooth the response and constrain its range so motion stays calm beside
  work.
- Keep `paused`, `loading`, `failed`, and `offline` visually distinct from a
  quiet passage in a `playing` track, both with and without motion.
- Provide a readable static equivalent when animations are disabled
  (`omarchy toggle animations` or the **Steady** variant).

The plugin measures loudness, three frequency bands, and onsets at 25 Hz from
its own stream and reports when measurements are unavailable. Tine renders
whole-comb audio motion in its **Resonance** and **Impulse** variants while
staying mindful of bar redraw cost; see
[Surfaces](surfaces.md#what-it-costs-to-animate).

## Open proposals and questions

Each item below has a working default in the plugin and is straightforward to
adjust:

- **The listening pass.** All 451 tracks were indexed from collection
  metadata and have not yet been auditioned end-to-end for coding suitability,
  distracting speech or silence, or whether both initial collections should
  stay as-is.
- **The mood names.** Thirteen proposed display names live in
  `catalog/categories.json`, each shortened from the upstream collection's
  label (for example, "Zen" for "Asian & Zen Lo-Fi", "Weather" for "Seasons,
  Rain & Weather", "Hybrid" for "Hybrid, World & Cinematic").
- **Scroll and media-key defaults**, including holding right-click while
  scrolling as the single-wheel mouse fallback.
- **MPRIS presence on the desktop.** Registering as an MPRIS player enables
  media keys, which also makes Omarchy's built-in media widget (when shown on
  the bar) display `mood` as an active player titled with the mood's name. On
  by default; `mediaKeys` turns it off.
- **Small behavioral defaults:** choosing a mood closes the popup; changing
  mood while paused drops the held track (reading `stopped` rather than
  `paused`); skip or back while stopped starts playback; a pause longer than
  ten minutes releases `mpv` and resumes by re-fetching from roughly the same
  position; per-mood history tracks songs whose audio started and clears on
  shell restart; back restarts the previous track from the beginning rather
  than resuming mid-track.
- **Real-world environment coverage:** real trackpad scroll deltas, vertical
  bars, multi-monitor setups, sleep/resume, Bluetooth or audio-device
  switches, and long-term hosting reliability on GitHub Releases and the
  Internet Archive.
